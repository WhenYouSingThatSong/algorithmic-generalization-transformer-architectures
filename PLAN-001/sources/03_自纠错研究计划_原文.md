Self correction in looped transformers

Research plan   October 2026

Recommended direction. Train a continuous looped transformer to recover from its own intermediate mistakes, then identify the computations that restore correct reasoning. Correction training already exists; the opportunity is to connect natural-error recovery to a causally tested mechanism.
[Cui and Ye 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08100), Emergent Search and Backtracking: Huginn changes sustained answer preferences across loops, sometimes from wrong to correct. This supports behavioral revision; answer flips alone do not establish detection or repair of a causally active intermediate mistake.

[Krishna et al 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26487), When Recursive Models Finish Computing: attention-based TRMs often solve later and suppress particular latent perturbations after completion. Jacobian analysis and interventions support a dynamical recovery account, without identifying a semantic error-repair algorithm.
[Liang et al 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33144), Beyond the Training Horizon: causal interventions distinguish represented values from their computational usability; native-error cohorts show transient correct readouts without sustained recovery. [Zhou et al 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19768), Learning to Compute Is Not Learning to Retain or Reuse: retaining a readable answer does not ensure reusable state; restoring a fresh source and its receiving state can recover one subsequent computation. These are model-specific limits, not evidence that correction is impossible.

[Ren and Liu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10679), Reasoning or Guessing: HRM trajectories can linger near incorrect attractors. This motivates distinguishing semantic repair from eventual escape or a new solution attempt.
Closest direct precedent: [Altabaa et al 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14095), Unlocking Out-of-Distribution Generalization, corrupts discrete latent values and supervises their correction across recurrent iterations. Its detailed mechanistic analysis uses the model without correction training, leaving the learned repair computation unresolved.

[TRM 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871) trains repeated answer refinement with deep supervision; [Deep Improvement Supervision 2025–2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16886) supervises progressively better intermediate answers. [Equilibrium Reasoners 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21488) uses randomized initialization and trajectory noise to encourage robust solution attractors. These are relevant recursive baselines; distinguish attention-based variants from MLP variants and improvement from specific error repair. 

[Tiny Recursive Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871) train repeated refinement of latent reasoning and answer representations using deep supervision. 

Maybe: [CaLR: Causal Latent Revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20981)  
[SCoRe 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12917) uses staged multi-turn RL to learn correction while avoiding behavioral collapse. [RISE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18219) learns improved continuations of learner-generated attempts. Their transferable principle is to train on errors the current model actually makes; feedback and stopping protocols must remain explicit.

[Phan et al 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16331), Decoding the Critique Mechanism, steers a critique direction to improve detection after textual arithmetic corruption. [Kumaran et al 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22271), Internal Confidence Signals, causally rescues error detection using an evaluative representation; correction success is also probed. Conversely, [Yuan et al 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09502), Hidden Error Awareness, finds that diagnostic probes do not reliably yield corrective control. These study textual models, not autonomous repair inside looped transformers.

Primary hypothesis. Training on model-generated mistakes will improve sustained recovery beyond generic noise training; some failures will additionally require restoring computational status, such as which operation is pending and which intermediate is usable. Treat this as a hypothesis, not an established mechanism.
Use a small transformer with a shared recurrent block, fixed access to the input, and an editable latent workspace. Keep this backbone constant across the first comparisons. Start with finite-state execution and modular-arithmetic graphs, where a simulator supplies exact intermediate targets and counterfactual continuations. Hold out longer chains and unseen compositions; reserve a second task family for transfer. Use at least three training seeds.
Warm-start on clean problems. Then mix clean runs with runs containing one controlled error. Compare Gaussian noise with semantic corruption: replace a value using a donor state matched for task progress, or intervene on an independently validated progress/status feature. Include value-only, status-only and combined faults; random-vector noise is a separate control.

Continue for a fixed repair budget and optimize final-answer loss plus intermediate semantic losses on the repaired value and its downstream consequences. Include clean-run supervision so correct states remain useful. Supervise semantic progress separately from loop count, since repair may revisit an earlier step. Avoid forcing every latent vector to match a teacher vector; equivalent computations can use different representations. This extends Altabaa-style training to continuous states.
Collect native failed trajectories from training problems, save the state near the first identifiable error, and train continuations from those states using simulator-derived targets. Keep confidently wrong and uncertain states separate. Refresh the collection as weights change; old hidden states may become incompatible with the current representation. Mix natural errors with injected errors and clean cases. This adapts the learner-rollout principle of RISE and SCoRe through supervised backpropagation across repair loops.

Use a fixed inference budget without oracle error flags. An optional critic may predict whether and where revision is needed, but it must prove useful through intervention. If later using RL, define sampled edit/checkpoint actions or an explicit stochastic latent policy; deterministic hidden updates do not directly supply policy-gradient log probabilities.
If value repair fails because the computation cannot revisit an earlier step, add a small latent checkpoint memory and a learned continue, revisit or halt controller. Train restoration of both source content and receiving-state readiness. Ablate memory and controller separately. For pretrained models with textual teachers, an alternative is to distill mistake–critique–repair traces into semantic latent transitions; retain error-free traces as a matched control.
Compare clean-only training, Gaussian-noise training, semantic-corruption training, and semantic-plus-native-error training, with the same intermediate supervision and matched training compute. Add a discrete-state counterpart and a deep-improvement baseline as resources allow. At inference, compare equal compute spent on extra loops, restart and the proposed repair method; count critic and checkpoint costs.

Proceed to a larger pretrained looped model only if gains persist on held-out native errors and new compositions, preserve initial-answer accuracy, and outperform equal-compute controls. Report matched task and semantic-error cohorts as well as each model’s own errors, so easier error cohorts cannot manufacture an apparent recovery gain.

Gap: early wrong readouts and late correct answers do not identify a repaired mistake. First show that the suspect intermediate causally affects later computation through matched state swaps. Then test whether the model restores the correct intermediate and its downstream consequences. Compare with uninterrupted extra computation, restarting from the input, and readout-only changes. Align traces by semantic progress rather than assuming one loop equals one reasoning step.
Gap: correction training has not yet yielded a settled causal account of semantic repair in continuous looped models. Test two competing explanations: an error-sensitive control process that redirects computation, and distributed recurrent dynamics that recompute or denoise without an explicit detector. Ablate candidate heads or subspaces on matched correct and erroneous runs; patch them back to test rescue. Probe accuracy alone is insufficient, and indiscriminate damage is not correction-specific evidence.
Gap: prior interventions establish state-usability constraints, but not learned autonomous restoration. Cross value-only versus status-only corruption with content-only versus status-only repair, then test joint repair. Measure whether restoration enables the next correct operation and a sustained correct suffix. This directly tests whether an apparently corrected value remains unusable until routing, freshness or the receiving position is reset.
Gap: recovery from artificial noise may not transfer to the model’s own errors. Compare Gaussian, semantic and native-error training under matched supervision, then evaluate unseen fault locations, error types, longer horizons and a second task. Report native-error performance separately from injected-error performance. Test whether discrete bottlenecks help recovery or mainly prevent errors from arising.
Gap: better final accuracy can hide harmful revisions or extra computation. Measure recovery probability conditional on an error, damage to initially correct runs, sustained intermediate recovery, final accuracy and compute to recovery. Use a fixed maximum budget or a learned stopping rule without test-time ground truth. Include correct cases resembling the error cases to detect an overactive critic.
Pilot: establish causal intermediate-state measurements and benchmark the clean, Gaussian and semantic training conditions on one task. Main experiment: add refreshed native-error training and the content/status factorial intervention; replicate across seeds and a second task. Extension: introduce checkpoint control or transfer to a pretrained model only after a consistent benefit appears. Reserve validation data for corruption rates, loss weights and stopping thresholds.

Target contribution. A defensible result would show that training improves sustained recovery from native latent errors, identify the recurrent operations responsible, and explain when recovery fails. Avoid a broad first-self-correction claim: the novelty must lie in the demonstrated mechanism, generalization or controlled comparison. Most recent evidence is from preprints and narrow tasks; treat the proposed gap as a literature-based assessment, not proof of absence.
